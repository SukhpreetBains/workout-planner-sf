Headline

The three questions, in the order a reader asks them.

Question 1

Did it do the job right?

“Those are two of the important metrics, and the third one is, and it did the right thing. If we can validate those three things somehow, I feel like that is our test suite, or that’s our sales pitch.” Calvin O’Keefe, 2026-08-21
Question 2

What did it cost, and was that predictable?

“Is it as token efficient in between runs? Like how different is the cost between each run? Is it, when you do like the PowerPoint one, is it 70 credits one time and 40 credits another time?” Calvin O’Keefe, 2026-08-21

Both halves are required. A cheap average with a wide spread is a worse product than a slightly dearer run that lands in the same place every time, because consistency is what is being sold.

Question 3

How much time did it save?

Instead of it just being some vague, like, oh, it feels like it took an 8 point story to a 5 point story.” Calvin O’Keefe, 2026-08-21
