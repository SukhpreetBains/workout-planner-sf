**Prepared by:** Alonso Reyes (Accountable Executive) with input from the
pursuit team
**For:** Discovery delivery team
**Date:** 28 August 2026
**Status:** Final — handed off at the pursuit-to-delivery transition call

This is the context we built during the pursuit. It is what we told Helio we

---

## Why Helio is doing this now

Helio's growth plan through 2029 depends on two things their current commercial
estate cannot support: entering three new geographic markets, and moving a
meaningful share of consumables and service revenue to a recurring model.

Both were raised unprompted by Dr. Brandt in the second pursuit meeting. His
framing, close to verbatim: *"We are being asked to grow into markets our
systems have never seen, with a commercial model our systems were never built
for. I can hire around that for one year. I cannot hire around it for five."*

There is also a compliance dimension. Helio had an internal audit finding in
Q1 2026 relating to pricing approval traceability. It was remediated with a
manual control. Marta Kovač has been explicit that a manual control is not an
acceptable steady state, and the audit finding is part of why IT sponsorship
exists at EVP level rather than delegated down.

## Strategic goals (Helio's own language)

These four came off Helio's FY26 commercial strategy deck, which Camille Okafor
shared during the pursuit. We should use their words, not ours.

1. **Build execution credibility** — deliver what they commit to, on the date
   they commit to it.
2. **Growth in key markets** — the three new geographies plus depth in existing.
3. **Simplify ways of working** — explicitly aimed at reducing the internal cost
   of getting a quote out the door.
4. **Best-in-class customer experience** — hospital administrators and
   biomedical engineering teams as the customer, not just the surgeon.

## Current commercial challenges

Framed at the level the Steering Committee talks about:

- Aging and inflexible systems do not scale well for growth.
- Current processes and data quality hamper both customer and user experience.
- Lack of controls and data integrity issues increase compliance risk.

## Pain points

The specific, operational complaints we heard repeatedly across the pursuit.
These came from interviews with eleven people across sales, service and IT.

- Systems are not industry-standard and cannot scale to support growth.
- Poor data quality, poor visibility, and lack of reporting inhibit
  decision-making.
- Potential risk of compliance violations (see the Q1 audit finding above).
- Manual, redundant work is costly and leads to errors.
- An inflexible, highly customized system limits agility — Helio IT quoted
  "nine to fourteen months" for any material change to the current CRM.

## What we have NOT established

Flagging these because they will be asked about at kickoff and we do not have
answers:

- **No baseline metrics.** We have anecdotes about quote cycle time and case
  handling time but no measured baseline for anything. Camille has offered to
  pull what her analytics team has. Until that lands we have no numbers we can
  responsibly show a client.
- **No agreed success criteria.** The SOW deliverable list includes a Project
  Charter that will carry success criteria; those criteria do not exist yet and
  are to be developed jointly in the first two weeks.
- **Data quality is asserted, not measured.** Everyone says data quality is bad.
  Nobody has run an assessment. This is a real gap for migration planning.
