# Health & Life Sciences — overview

A domain primer, not a methodology. Use this to interpret what a client
says, not to change how any Ascent skill works.

## Who "HLS" covers

- **Providers** — health systems, hospitals, physician groups, care
  delivery networks. Workstreams cluster around clinical operations,
  patient access and experience, care coordination, and revenue cycle.
- **Payers** — health plans and insurers. Workstreams cluster around
  member experience, claims and prior authorization, provider network
  management, and risk adjustment.
- **Pharma / biotech / medtech (life sciences)** — workstreams cluster
  around clinical trials and R&D, regulatory submissions, commercial and
  market access, and manufacturing/supply chain.

A single engagement often spans more than one of these (e.g., a payer
building a provider-facing portal). Don't force a client into exactly
one bucket — name the specific mix in scope.

## Common workstream types

- Clinical operations and care coordination
- Patient / member experience and access
- Revenue cycle management (RCM)
- Interoperability and EHR/EMR integration
- Regulatory affairs and compliance
- Clinical trials and R&D data (life sciences)
- Population health and value-based care

## Common stakeholder archetypes

Useful when a document names a role without much context: CMIO / CNIO
(clinical informatics leadership), CNO (nursing operations), care
manager, revenue cycle / RCM lead, payer operations lead, regulatory
affairs lead, compliance officer, HIM (health information management)
lead, clinical trial coordinator.

## Regulatory landscape touchpoints

Not an exhaustive compliance reference — enough to recognize when a
document is making a regulatory claim worth citing precisely rather than
paraphrasing: HIPAA (privacy and security of PHI), HITECH, the ONC/CMS
Interoperability and Patient Access rules, FDA regulations (life
sciences), CMS reimbursement and quality-reporting rules. See
[vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md) for the acronyms themselves.
