# Gap-Driven Interview Guide

Ask only about unresolved, high-value fields. Prefer plain project language over schema terminology.

## Decision rubric

Ask when all three are true:

1. The answer affects which sources a later skill will read.
2. The answer cannot be inferred confidently from high-signal metadata.
3. A wrong assumption would waste context, miss authoritative material, or cross an access boundary.

Do not ask merely to make the manifest exhaustive.

## High-value prompts

Use or adapt one compact batch:

- “What outcome are you working toward right now, and what phase is the project in?”
- “Which files or folders are canonical for requirements, decisions, and current status? I found `<candidate>`; should I record it?”
- “Are there Slack channels, Teams chats/channels, or SharePoint folders that later tasks should treat as project context? Names or links are enough.”

When a source is dense, ask for a narrowing cue:

- relevant date range
- topic, feature, workstream, or decision terms
- key authors or owners
- whether threads, attachments, or meeting notes matter

## Stop conditions

Stop interviewing when the manifest contains:

- a clear current objective
- at least one canonical requirements or decision source
- the implementation workspace or an explicit statement that none exists
- enough location detail to resolve each remote source
- explicit open questions for anything important that remains unknown

Do not block completion on optional fields such as a complete stakeholder directory or exhaustive terminology.
