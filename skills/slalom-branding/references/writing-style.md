# Writing Style

Source: *Slalom Brand Guide*, February 2025, pp. 20-23. Slalom follows *The Chicago Manual of Style* (CMoS) and *Merriam-Webster*, bending CMoS in the few places noted below. The full glossary lives in the Slalom writing style guide.

## Voice

Clear, confident, conversational. People-first not technology-first; uplifting not authoritative; friendly not formal; real not slick; innovative not expected.

- Appeal to the reader's emotions and needs; address them directly in second person ("you can," not "the client can").
- The customer is the hero. Frame benefits from their perspective, not Slalom's.
- Write crisply. Explain it the way you would to a friend. If a simple sentence works, use it.
- Avoid jargon; define any necessary term on first use.
- Prefer active voice. Back arguments with metrics and cite sources.

## Preferred terms

- **Customers,** not clients — including "our customers' customers."
- **Slalomers** in colloquial, informal settings. Never "Slalomites." Use **Slalom team members** for formal and external communications.
- Never separate "fiercely" from "human," never call something "fierce," and never imply Slalom makes people more human. See [messaging.md](messaging.md).

## Capitalization

- **Sentence case** for headlines and subheads across every medium — website, decks, SharePoint alike. Title case only in narrow cases such as Medium blogs and wired press releases.
- **Offering and solution names:** capitalize true proper nouns (Moonshot, Build Centers, Salesforce Community Cloud); leave self-explanatory descriptors lowercase (our experience design practice, customer data platform).
- **Job titles:** lowercase in body copy (senior consultant, managing director); standard capitalization in pull quotes, social assets, and formal settings (Solution Principal, General Manager).
- **Industries:** lowercase in general usage (healthcare, financial services). Capitalize in industry lists or where clarity demands.

## Abbreviations and acronyms

Spell out on first reference with the abbreviation in parentheses if reused: American Cancer Society (ACS), minimum viable product (MVP). No periods between letters for most acronyms (CEO, US, UK). The spelled-out term is often lowercase even when the acronym is capitalized (AI is artificial intelligence).

## Numbers and punctuation

- Spell out zero through nine; use digits for 10 and up. Flexible in headlines and social, and for large financial metrics ($7K, a $1B company).
- **Percent:** numerals plus `%` in most cases — a deliberate break from CMoS — except at the start of a sentence. "The team reported a 44% increase." / "Ninety percent of cats are silently correcting your grammar."
- **Em dash:** closed, no spaces around it. "Three writers—Ruth, Randi, and David—agreed." Do not use spaced en dashes.
- **Quotation marks:** use curly smart quotes in image text, not slanted or straight quotes.
- **Spaces:** one space between sentences and after colons, never two.

## Inclusive language

Avoid biased language and idioms with a problematic history. Do not default to he/him/his for universal statements — singular "they" is widely accepted. When writing about an individual, use their pronouns.
