# Photography and Illustration

Source: *Slalom Brand Guide*, February 2025, pp. 61-82. Judge every image against [creative-lens.md](creative-lens.md).

## Photography direction

- **Tone:** positive, optimistic, joyful. **Scenes:** authentic, bright, colorful. **Color:** vibrant and saturated, balanced by neutrals, with pops of the brand palette. **Subjects:** real people and places, high-quality, low-pretension, candid rather than posed.
- Avoid dark or moody scenes, cyberpunk or dystopian imagery, generic technology visuals, glamorized or overly posed stock, US-only representation, motion blur, and camera effects such as light leaks, lens flares, and tilt shifts.

## Images of people

Three styles:

1. **Slalom employees** — collaboration and core values in authentic, bright scenes. Candid expressions, real interactions. Caption real people where possible. Do not crop faces or read as corporate.
2. **Global perspective** — customers, their customers, partners, and communities, shot documentary-style and culturally accurate down to fashion and wall outlets. On somber subjects, find authentic hope while keeping a respectful lens.
3. **Portraits** — captured in the moment. Add slight warmth to flatter skin tones, brand color in clothing, accessories, or props, and captions where possible. No corporate clichés.

Be diverse and inclusive across a set: vary age, skin tone, ability, and geography rather than repeating one profile.

## Other subjects

- **Places:** daylight, known landmarks, regional architecture or native vegetation, expansive skies, and a touch of human presence. Avoid stylized night scenes.
- **Industries and capabilities:** technically accurate — check that the wind turbines are right. Never generic, computer-generated, or overly futuristic.
- **Backgrounds:** simple enough for text and logo legibility, showcasing a single brand color. Recoloring is allowed; a solid filter over the whole photo is not.
- **Conceptual:** clever and artistic, not obtuse. Consider the audience's culture. Avoid clichés like hands forming a heart.

## Working with images

**Adding brand color:** adjust wardrobe, accessories, props, and objects toward the palette, and enhance overall vibrancy and saturation. Never radically alter colors in documentary-style photography — a black shirt does not become red, and a color carrying real-world meaning stays as shot. Start from the "True Color" preset in the Creative Library.

**Captions:** add them when representing a genuine Slalom employee, team, event, or office; a caption can be a name and title or a full sentence. Skip them when a headline already names the subject, or when the Slalom logo is visible and no individual needs highlighting.

**Stock:** only when a custom shoot is not feasible, and **never to depict a Slalom employee**. Real employees require a signed release form.

**Image stories:** sequence logically, open with the most arresting visual, vary wide/medium/close shots, caption for context, and match artistic style, lighting, and color grade across the set.

## Illustration

Bundled library: `assets/illustrations/` (116 SVGs, 37 subjects, indexed in its README).

- The brand is photo-first. Use illustration as an accent, not the primary subject, and avoid overusing it. A project leaning heavily on illustration should go through the Global Marketing Studio.
- Approved illustration colors are black, Slalom Blue, and Slalom Dark Blue. Recolor by importing the SVG, selecting it, and applying a brand swatch; in InDesign, open the SVG in Illustrator first and paste it in.
- Show a diverse assortment of people — skin tones, body shapes, ages, genders. Vary the skin-tone variants across a deliverable rather than repeating one figure.
- Do not create net-new illustrations, icons, or logos.
